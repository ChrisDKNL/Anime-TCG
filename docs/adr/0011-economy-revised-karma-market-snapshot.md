# 0011 — Economy revised: Karma, a player market, a pack shop, five fixed tiers, a snapshot pool

2026-10-29 · accepted · amends ADR 0004 (supersedes §1's second sentence, §3's sources of currency and §4's sell and trade rules); ADR 0004 §2, §5, §6 and §7 (as amended by ADR 0008 §6) stand

## Context

The first drafts of `docs/design/collection-packs.md` (1c) and `docs/design/economy.md` (item 2) left their open questions to the owner. The owner answered them on 2026-10-29. Several answers reverse rules of ADR 0004, which forbade buying packs with currency, priced every sale by the system and limited trade to local profiles on one device (a limit ADR 0008 had already made obsolete by moving accounts to the server). ADR 0004 is not edited after acceptance (`docs/adr/README.md`), so this record amends it. The owner's words, quoted in both specs' decision records as "Owner, 2026-10-29": packs keep a free one every 10 minutes and add themed packs bought with the in-game currency, "different packs that have different collections of characters"; all cards can be sold, including the last copy, on a player-to-player market; the currency is called Karma; rarity is fixed per character by its popularity; the pool is a snapshot so AniList changes never influence the live game, with an owner-run option to update it. In a second round the same day the owner set five tiers, "Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic" (keeping the name "Mythic"), their split and their odds per pack, grouped series into franchises, and reserved "Masterwork" versions of cards and "Relics" for later.

## Decision

1. **Packs may be bought with in-game Karma, never with money.** The free pack stays time-gated (one every 10 minutes, up to 3 stored, ADR 0004 §1 otherwise unchanged). Beside it, a Karma shop sells themed packs, each drawing from its own published set of characters, at fixed Karma prices with published odds. This supersedes ADR 0004 §1's "Packs cannot be bought with currency, directly or indirectly through a shop". No pack, card or Karma is ever sold for real money, and Karma is never convertible to it (`CLAUDE.md`, unchanged).
2. **The one currency is named Karma.** Integer-valued and in-game only, as ADR 0004 §3 says. It is earned by opening free packs (the faucet `economy.md` §6 sets), by selling cards to other players and, later, by winning games; it is spent on themed packs, market purchases, fees (market and recycle) and wagers. This supersedes ADR 0004 §3's "earned by selling cards to the system": there is no sale to the system.
3. **Cards are traded between server accounts on a player market, for Karma.** Any card can be listed at a price its owner sets within published bounds, and another account buys it; every card can be sold, the last copy included. Karma therefore moves between accounts, through the market only, with a fee, bounds and limits against one person farming across accounts (`economy.md` §10). This supersedes ADR 0004 §4's _sell_ ("card → currency, fixed by rarity") and its _trade_ limited to local profiles on one device. _Recycle_ stays: five copies of one character become one random character of the next rarity, with a fee.
4. **Rarity is fixed per character by popularity, in five tiers: Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic.** Each character in the pool has one tier, set by its rank in AniList favourites within the pool: Mythic the top 1 %, Legendary the next 4 %, Epic 10 %, Rare 25 %, Common the remaining 60 %. A pack rolls each slot's tier with published odds, which the owner sets as the chance that a five-card pack holds at least one card of the tier (Rare 94.1 %, Epic 17.6 %, Legendary 2 %, Mythic 0.33 %, Common filling the rest of each slot), then draws a character of that tier uniformly. This replaces the proof of concept's three tiers and its popularity tilt (per-card odds bent by popularity). A card keeps the rarity it was granted with for good, whatever later snapshots decide; names and images follow the newest snapshot.
5. **The character pool is an owner-managed AniList snapshot.** It holds every MAIN and SUPPORTING character of the 100 most popular anime on AniList, taken by an owner-run command that is the only code calling AniList, and versioned. Its anime are grouped into franchises through AniList's sequel, prequel and side-story relations, and series completion counts per franchise. The live game never calls AniList and never refreshes on its own; the owner may take a new snapshot later, which changes future packs, tiers and completion totals but never an owned card's rarity.
6. **Collection reset is an owner command**, like the password reset: no player-facing reset exists.
7. **Masterwork cards and Relics are reserved, not designed.** A Masterwork is a variant of a character's card with a rarity above the character's tier; a Relic is a separate kind of collectible. Neither has rules yet. The data model and the contract keep a card `variant` (only `standard` today) and an item `kind` (only `card` today) so both can arrive later without a breaking migration; each needs its own spec, and any change to the economy rules for them goes through this ADR's successor.

## Consequences

- `docs/design/collection-packs.md` (1c) and `docs/design/economy.md` (item 2) are revised to these rules the same day; each carries the owner's decisions in a decision record and the remaining choices as open questions.
- ADR 0004 §5 (the loop must not inflate) now has to hold with a Karma shop and a market: `economy.md` §9 shows the faucet tied to the pack timer, the shop as the main sink, fees as further sinks, bounded prices and a linear bound on growth.
- The market is a new trust boundary: one account's request moves another account's Karma and cards. Every market PR is T3, with double-buy, self-trade, price-tampering and deadlock tests, and the threat model in `docs/05-security.md` gains multi-account farming with its residual risk stated.
- Roadmap item 7 (trade) narrows to card-for-card swaps, if ever; cards for Karma arrive with item 2. The manager updates `docs/02-roadmap.md`.
- The collection overview (`docs/design/collection-overview.md`) was designed for a sell-to-the-system action, a same-character recycle and a per-character-and-rarity entry; `ui-designer` revises it to the market, the random recycle, one entry per character, five tiers and franchises.
- Five tiers change every economy number: `economy.md` re-derives reference values, recycle fees for four steps (Mythic has no tier above), market bounds, the themed pack price and the model, and the market limits run over seven days so a Mythic can be bought. The UI needs two more rarity colour tokens (Epic, Mythic).
