# 0011 — Economy revised: Karma, a player market, a pack shop, five fixed tiers, a snapshot pool

2026-10-29 · accepted · amends ADR 0004 (supersedes §1's second sentence, §3's sources of currency, and §4's sell, recycle and trade rules); ADR 0004 §2, §5, §6 and §7 (as amended by ADR 0008 §6) stand

## Context

The first drafts of `docs/design/collection-packs.md` (1c) and `docs/design/economy.md` (item 2) left their open questions to the owner, who answered them in three rounds on 2026-10-29. Several answers reverse rules of ADR 0004, which forbade buying packs with currency, priced every sale by the system, made recycling an upgrade, and limited trade to local profiles on one device (a limit ADR 0008 had already made obsolete by moving accounts to the server). ADR 0004 is not edited after acceptance (`docs/adr/README.md`), so this record amends it. The owner's words, quoted in both specs' decision records as "Owner, 2026-10-29":

- Round 1: packs keep a free one every 10 minutes and add themed packs bought with the in-game currency, "different packs that have different collections of characters"; all cards can be sold, including the last copy, on a player-to-player market; the currency is called Karma; rarity is fixed per character by its popularity; the pool is a snapshot so AniList changes never influence the live game, with an owner-run option to update it.
- Round 2: five tiers, "Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic" (keeping the name "Mythic"), their split and their odds per pack; series grouped into franchises; "Masterwork" versions of cards and "Relics" reserved for later.
- Round 3: the Rare odds lowered so a given Rare character is rarer than a given Common one; no tier recycling at all, cards can only be recycled for Karma; Karma comes from recycling duplicate cards, never the last copy, at 25 % of the tier's reference value; upgrades happen only through the mini-games, for example "an ante of five Legendary cards that results in a Mythic upgrade if the game is won".

## Decision

1. **Packs may be bought with in-game Karma, never with money.** The free pack stays time-gated (one every 10 minutes, up to 3 stored, ADR 0004 §1 otherwise unchanged). Beside it, a Karma shop sells themed packs, each drawing from its own published set of characters, at fixed Karma prices with published odds. This supersedes ADR 0004 §1's "Packs cannot be bought with currency, directly or indirectly through a shop". No pack, card or Karma is ever sold for real money, and Karma is never convertible to it (`CLAUDE.md`, unchanged).
2. **The one currency is named Karma.** Integer-valued and in-game only, as ADR 0004 §3 says. It enters the game only by recycling duplicate cards and, later, by winning games; it moves between players on the market; it is spent on themed packs, market purchases, the market fee and wagers. Recycling a duplicate is a sale to the system at a fixed price, a quarter of the tier's reference value, and the system takes nothing else: the last copy of a character at a rarity never, and no card at a price of the player's choosing. This narrows ADR 0004 §3's "earned by selling cards to the system" to duplicates at that fixed price; every other sale is to another player.
3. **Cards are traded between server accounts on a player market, for Karma.** Any card can be listed at a price its owner sets within published bounds, and another account buys it; every card can be sold, the last copy included. Karma therefore moves between accounts, through the market only, with a fee, bounds and limits against one person farming across accounts (`economy.md` §10). This supersedes ADR 0004 §4's _sell_ ("card → currency, fixed by rarity"), which survives only as the recycling of duplicates (§4), and its _trade_ limited to local profiles on one device.
4. **Recycling turns duplicates into Karma, and nothing else.** A duplicate (any copy beyond the last one of a character at a rarity) recycles for a set Karma value per tier, a quarter of its reference value rounded down and at least 1; the last copy is never recycled, nor a copy on the market or held as an ante. There is no tier recycling: several cards never become a better card by recycling. This supersedes ADR 0004 §4's _recycle_ ("several cards → one upgraded card or reroll") and this ADR's own first version ("five copies become one random character of the next rarity").
5. **Upgrades happen only through the mini-games** (roadmap items 5 and 6), for example an ante of five Legendary cards that becomes a Mythic if the game is won. Item 2 builds none of it; the card model reserves what an ante needs (cards held in escrow for the length of a round, unavailable to list or recycle), and the games' specs set the rules, the odds and their house edge.
6. **Rarity is fixed per character by popularity, in five tiers: Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic.** Each character in the pool has one tier, set by its rank in AniList favourites within the pool: Mythic the top 1 %, Legendary the next 4 %, Epic 10 %, Rare 25 %, Common the remaining 60 %. A pack rolls each slot's tier with published odds, which the owner sets as the chance that a five-card pack holds at least one card of the tier (Rare 80 %, Epic 17.6 %, Legendary 2 %, Mythic 0.33 %, Common filling the rest of each slot), so that each tier's characters are individually rarer than the tier below; then it draws a character of that tier uniformly. This replaces the proof of concept's three tiers and its popularity tilt. A card keeps the rarity it was granted with for good, whatever later snapshots decide; names and images follow the newest snapshot.
7. **The character pool is an owner-managed AniList snapshot.** It holds every MAIN and SUPPORTING character of the 100 most popular anime on AniList, taken by an owner-run command that is the only code calling AniList, and versioned. Its anime are grouped into franchises through AniList's sequel, prequel and side-story relations, and series completion counts per franchise. The live game never calls AniList and never refreshes on its own; the owner may take a new snapshot later, which changes future packs, tiers and completion totals but never an owned card's rarity.
8. **Collection reset is an owner command**, like the password reset: no player-facing reset exists. The owner chooses per reset whether the account's Karma is kept; by default it goes to 0.
9. **Masterwork cards and Relics are reserved, not designed.** A Masterwork is a variant of a character's card with a rarity above the character's tier; a Relic is a separate kind of collectible. Neither has rules yet. The data model and the contract keep a card `variant` (only `standard` today) and an item `kind` (only `card` today) so both can arrive later without a breaking migration; each needs its own spec, and any change to the economy rules for them goes through this ADR's successor.

## Consequences

- `docs/design/collection-packs.md` (1c) and `docs/design/economy.md` (item 2) carry the owner's three rounds in their decision records and have no open questions left.
- ADR 0004 §5 (the loop must not inflate) now has to hold with a Karma shop, a market and a duplicate recycler: `economy.md` §9 shows the faucet bounded by the cards the pack timer deals, the shop as the main sink (its cards recycle for about a tenth of its price), the market fee as a further sink, no buy-to-recycle arbitrage, bounded prices and a linear bound on growth.
- The market is a new trust boundary: one account's request moves another account's Karma and cards. Every market PR is T3, with double-buy, self-trade, price-tampering and deadlock tests, and the threat model in `docs/05-security.md` gains multi-account farming with its residual risk stated.
- Roadmap item 7 (trade) narrows to card-for-card swaps, if ever; cards for Karma arrive with item 2. Upgrades move to items 5 and 6. The manager updates `docs/02-roadmap.md`.
- The collection overview (`docs/design/collection-overview.md`) and `docs/design/market-and-shop.md` follow these rules: one entry per character, five tiers and franchises, List on the market, and Recycle for Karma (duplicates only) in place of any upgrade. The UI needs two more rarity colour tokens (Epic, Mythic).
- Five tiers and the round-3 rules set every economy number: `economy.md` derives reference values from the per-slot odds, recycle values as a quarter of them, market bounds, the themed pack price and the model, and the market limits run over seven days, sized so one Mythic at the upper bound can be bought.
